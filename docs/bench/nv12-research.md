# Lossless NV12 cache research

2026-11-19 · A 3840×2160 NV12 frame has 12,441,600 active bytes (11.87 MiB). FrameScrub needs any cached frame in either scrub direction and exposes its decoded planes to existing consumers. In the M4 Max baseline at a 256 MiB cap, Apple Compression LZ4 raised capacity from 21 to 35–39 frames on Jockey and Bosphorus, with 4.72–5.06 ms p95 restore time and 17–18 ms CPU to fill. These are the relevant local controls. [Decoded-frame cache benchmark](decoded-frame-cache.md) · [Eight-mode NV12 benchmark](nv12-cache-benchmark.md)

## Small independent blocks

- Tikekar et al. compressed already reconstructed 8-bit HEVC reference frames using separate 4×4 luma and chroma blocks. Each compressed block stores its minimum and uniform-width unsigned offsets. An index stores one base address and 24 block widths, so a small prefix sum locates any block. The authors report 1.2–5× compression across 384 HEVC test runs and 50% average reduction. Their 40 nm HEVC decoder with embedded DRAM was measured on 1920×1080 footage at 24–50 fps. The ratios depend on texture and source quantization, and the chip throughput covers a hardware decoding pipeline. [Tikekar et al., IEEE JSSC 2018, §§III-A and V](https://eems.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/2018_jssc_wearable_hevc.pdf)
- Guo et al. used 8×8 luma and 4×4 chroma blocks, directional differences and averaging, and groups of residuals packed at a common width. They report 2.49× on luma for 18 reconstructed HEVC sequences and a synthesized 300 MHz ASIC pipeline rated up to 3.2 Gsamples/s encode and 6.4 Gsamples/s decode. The luma ratio measures payload efficiency on that suite. The ASIC rate measures a dedicated parallel design and offers no CPU latency estimate. [Guo et al., IEEE Transactions on Multimedia 2014, §§II–IV](https://viplab.fudan.edu.cn/vip/attachments/download/6964/A%20New%20Reference%20Frame%20Recompression%20Algorithm%20and%20Its%20VLSI%20Architecture%20for%20UHDTV%20Video%20Codec.pdf)
- SIMD-BP128 uses per-width unpackers and interleaves groups so masks and shifts restore multiple values at once. Lemire and Boytsov report about 2.3 billion 32-bit integers/s on a 3.4 GHz Core i7-2600. They benchmarked sorted integer lists on x86, so this establishes a SIMD layout precedent. NV12's byte samples, signed residuals and two-dimensional locality need their own measurements. [Lemire and Boytsov, Table I and §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.2137)

A first CPU experiment can compare minimum-plus-offset with a reversible row predictor, starting at 4×4 or 8×8. Keep U and V independent for prediction and restore to interleaved UV. Charge widths, offsets, raw fallback and padding to the cap. Minimum-plus-offset supports independent sample reads after finding the block. Row prediction generally needs a bounded reconstruction scan and may be more suitable when restoring the whole block.

## Lightweight codec controls

- Use Apple framed LZ4 as the existing baseline and upstream raw LZ4 or Apple `COMPRESSION_LZ4_RAW` as a matching control. A framed Apple LZ4 block may reference the previous block. Give each frame or tile its own stream if that unit needs independent access. Try reversible row filtering with the same LZ4 implementation to isolate the filter's contribution. The QOIR author measured filtered LZ4PNG using about 85% of the unfiltered LZ4PNG bytes and 57% of its decode throughput on the QOI RGB/RGBA suite, single-threaded on an i5-10210U. It gives a useful trade-off to measure on NV12 without converting the frame to RGB. [Apple LZ4 framing](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/compression/compression_lz4) · [QOIR benchmark and host](https://github.com/nigeltao/qoir/blob/main/doc/full_benchmarks.txt)
- ZXC’s author reports 11.01 GB/s decode and 290 MB/s encode at level 3 versus 4.94 GB/s and 873 MB/s for LZ4 1.11.0. The respective payloads were 46.65% and 47.32% of raw input. This was an Apple M3, one thread, 512 KB blocks and a Silesia archive. Level 2 improves fill to 652 MB/s and decode to 12.42 GB/s, with 53.86% payload. The fill cost and size both matter when frames are continually entering and leaving the cache. Apple’s buffer-only SIMD-oriented LZBITMAP is another local control where the runtime supports it. [ZXC whitepaper, §§7 and 7.5.1](https://libzxc.org/docs/whitepaper/#751-arm64-architecture-apple-silicon-m3) · [Apple LZBITMAP](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/compression/compression_lzbitmap)

## GPU access and memory accounting

- Arm’s AFBC supports lossless fine-grained access including an NV12 layout. It demonstrates direct hardware sampling of compressed planes. Apple documents lossless 8-bit bi-planar Core Video formats (`&8v0`, `&8f0`), and Chromium excludes those compressed surfaces from CPU access. Apple says Metal lossless texture compression reserves the full uncompressed texture size and may add metadata. Any Core Video capacity experiment needs actual allocation and physical footprint measurements alongside a bit-exact round trip. [Linux AFBC format](https://docs.kernel.org/gpu/afbc.html) · [Apple TN3121](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3121-selecting-a-pixel-format-for-an-avcapturevideodataoutput) · [Chromium IOSurface handling](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/master/ui/gfx/mac/io_surface.cc) · [Apple Metal texture data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/optimizing-texture-data)
- Guthe et al. indexed independent compressed bricks and decoded a brick cooperatively on demand. On a GTX TITAN X using CUDA 7.5, their 1080p volume renderer recorded over 98% hits in a small decompressed-brick cache and at least 40× speedup relative to the same decoder without that cache. The result measures reuse in volume ray casting. For FrameScrub, compare bulk Metal unpack into pooled NV12 plane textures with sampled tile reads only if the viewport can skip enough tiles. Measure through GPU completion, including queue latency and competition with rendering. [Guthe et al., GPU-Based Lossless Volume Data Compression, §§4–5](https://download.hrz.tu-darmstadt.de/pub/FB20/GCC/paper/Guthe-2016-LVC.pdf)

The external measurements give designs to test. The decision needs byte-exact Y and UV reconstruction with the original pixel range and attachments, actual resident bytes including restore buffers, fill CPU, random and reverse hit p95/p99, and hit-to-present latency on the same M4 Max footage and cache cap. Include textured or grainy footage because its larger local ranges can send many blocks to raw fallback.
