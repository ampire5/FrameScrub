# Posters: how many at a time

Date: 2026-10-26.

**Machine.** Apple M4 Max, 14 physical cores, macOS 27.0.

**How.**
- Run with `FRAMESCRUB_POSTER_BENCH=1 swift test --filter PosterBench`.
- 24 files on the local SSD: copies of `h264_cfr_gop250.mp4` (1080p H.264) and `hevc10_hlg.mov` (4K HEVC 10-bit HLG).
- For each file, a keyframe near a tenth in (`KeyframeThumbnail.make`).
- Workers take the next file from a shared index. One warm-up file goes first.

| Workers | Time | Posters/s |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.42 s | 57 |
| 2 | 0.33 s | 74 |
| 3 | 0.22 s | 108 |
| 4 | 0.17 s | 138 |
| 6 | 0.23 s | 103 |

**Choice.**
- On this Mac's own disks: a quarter of the physical cores, 1…4 (`RecentThumbnailer.localWorkers`, 3 here). That is about 2× one worker, and leaves the decoders and the cores to the player.
- On a share: one at a time. Its reads are what a poster waits for, and the share's bandwidth goes to playback first (`network-mode`).
- Recent's thumbnails stay at one: there are 50 at most.

- A folder on the NAS over SMB.
